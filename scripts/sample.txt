STORY: My Own Son Sold My House
My name is Gerald. I am seventy-four years old. Twenty-two years ago I built
my son's house with my own two hands, plank by plank, so he would never know
the cold nights I knew as a boy.

Then one morning, my daughter-in-law listed it for sale. She put a sign in
my garden. My garden. With my name buried in the paperwork.

When I confronted my son, he laughed and said, "It was always our plan." His
plan. Not mine. So I took my keys off the wall of the house I built, and I
drove home to my trailer. And that night, I made one phone call to a lawyer
I had helped for free, years ago.

STORY: The Wedding They Held Without Me
I spent thirty-one years as the family baker. Every birthday, every wedding,
every funeral cake came from my kitchen, and I never charged them a single
dollar.

Last spring, my niece got married. I found out from a photograph on the
internet. They drove past my window with the dress in the back seat and
never once asked me to bake the cake.

So on the day of the wedding, I did something nobody expected. I delivered
a cake to them after all. It was the ugliest cake I had ever made, and it
had one message written across the top, in icing: "Congratulations on the
family that forgot you."
